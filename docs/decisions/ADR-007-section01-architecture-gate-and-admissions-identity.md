# ADR-007: Section 01 architecture gate and admission identity

- Status: Accepted product direction, 1 November 2026; executable admission contract pending DB03/D1 review.
- Owner: Younas Khan / yunusyosaf399.
- Source: owner's response to the recommended Section 01 architecture gate and CUR-07 A1-G1 proposal: “So complete what you recommend ... every students get it one roll no, one registration no or admissions no and unique id ... only one member can only create one application at a time no simultaneously application.”
- Scope: requirement and decision gate only. No SQL, migration, grant or hosted change is authorized by this ADR.

## Decision

Section 01 closes when its product boundaries, feature ownership/classification, dependency order, branch comparison, decision record and versioned design inventory are recorded. Per-feature executable migration, final physical ERD, tested branch composition and whole-system release acceptance are tracked in their owning later sections and final database release gate. Closing this architecture gate never reports those implementation tasks complete. The Section 01 backlog wording remains available for the final release audit; its implementation criteria are transferred, not erased.

The owner accepted the proposed CUR-07 A1-G1 product direction by instructing implementation of the recommendation after it was presented. Exams and Learning use a restricted trusted eligibility source; Exams preserve the eligible subject basis at a specified prepublication checkpoint; published Learning work retains its original recipients; Timetable sees effective Section subjects without individual choice rosters; later corrections do not rewrite published snapshots; permissions are fixed per consumer; ambiguous or stale new actions fail closed. Exams' exact checkpoint, late Learning access/backfill and consumer SQL/permission signatures remain technical integration gates. This is product direction, not a claim of installed CUR-07 SQL.

One accepted student has one immutable internal Student UUID, one school-facing Student ID and one official admission number according to the configured school numbering policy. Admission/application numbers are separate: an unapproved applicant may have an application number but no Student ID or admission number. All authoritative routing and commands use server-resolved UUIDs and currently authorized relationships; displayed roll/admission/student numbers are lookup identifiers, never proof of access. Server-side unique constraints and allocator locks, not UI disablement, enforce uniqueness and replay.

Roll number belongs to an accepted enrollment and its applicable School policy/namespace. A Student has at most one active PRIMARY placement at a given business instant, with one effective roll in that placement. On a later year promotion, Section or Campus move, the same Student UUID and Student ID remain; the new enrollment may have a different roll. Historic rolls are preserved and may have been used by other Students in other years/Sections under the approved policy. Do not impose a lifetime-global unique roll number.

For one applicant, only one nonterminal application may be open across the school project, including DRAFT/PENDING and stages awaiting Principal review. A second staff member's simultaneous create for the same verified applicant must resolve to the existing authorized application or a safe conflict, never create a second open application. A rejected/withdrawn closed attempt may be followed by a separately numbered application under school policy; a Person already bound to an accepted Student cannot start a duplicate new-student intake. Prior application and numbering history remains.

A verified Foundation Person binding can enforce this by a partial unique open-application key and a lock in the protected create command. DRAFT may precede verified Person binding under the existing approved P2-D01 decision; candidate duplicate detection then needs a private, normalized, server-controlled identity claim and serialized match/reservation path, with non-enumerating responses and protected evidence. Exact identity attributes, collision/correction handling, retention and constraint shape belong to DB03's P2-D02/P2-D07 design and require review. Without a reliable identity match, the application must enter a controlled identity-resolution queue; no system can guarantee two incomplete identity submissions describe the same human. Never make a client-entered name alone the uniqueness key or expose whether a private Person already exists.

“One member creates one application at a time” is applied to an authenticated staff principal's individual create request with a unique idempotency key and to the same applicant's open-application invariant. It is not a global one-staff-member-at-a-time lock across the whole school: two administrators can create applications for different children concurrently. Do not use a browser button or single session as the sole enforcement.

## Example

Administrator A begins Ahmad's application for 2026 while Administrator B clicks Create for Ahmad. Their protected commands serialize the same verified applicant/identity reservation. One creates the DRAFT; the other receives the existing authorized application or a generic conflict. Ahmad has no Student ID yet. After documents, Finance clearance and Principal approval, one atomic handoff creates one Student UUID, school Student ID, admission number and first enrollment with its roll. A retry returns the original result. In 2027 Ahmad moves to another campus: his permanent identifiers remain; a new enrollment and possibly a new roll retain the earlier roll history.

## Acceptance and follow-up

DB03/D1 must test same-key and different-key races, two staff on the same verified Person, two drafts before Person verification, cross-year active applications, ambiguous and corrected identity claims, approved/rejected terminal replay, cancellation/reapplication, admission/Student ID allocator collisions, Principal/Finance gates, placement and roll allocation, cross-campus disclosure, grant revocation, full transaction rollback and retained history. Exact indexes/constraints, identity claim retention, source locks and authorization require a reviewed migration and local disposable/CI proof. They are not satisfied by this decision record.
