**Included:** configured level/class and campus/year section offerings; capacity and roll policy concepts; student profile, lifecycle and enrollment progression; family/child relationships; employee and teacher profiles; class-teacher and subject-teacher assignment boundaries; typed scope-resolver contracts. These are conceptual responsibilities, not approved SQL relation names or final field sets. Details are in [the entity map](25_domain_package_01_conceptual_entity_map.md), [dependency order](26_domain_package_01_dependency_order.md) and [resolver contracts](../security/05_domain_scope_resolver_contracts.md).

**Foundation anchors remain unchanged:** the existing 33 tables, 12 School OS roles, 49 policies, 39 application `SECURITY DEFINER` functions, `app`/unexposed `app_private` split, `people`, `principals` and Auth bindings, school profile, campuses, rooms, academic years, complete-grant evaluator, approval core, audit/outbox, command receipts and `file_objects`. D1A proposes no modification to `current_principal_id()` or `has_complete_grant(...)`. A campus is a school-internal scope; one school remains one Supabase project. Future domain entities refer to these anchors and must pass the established default-deny, protected-command and actor-attribution model.

**Excluded:** full admissions workflow, Finance/admission fee, curriculum/elective selections and assessment components pending the subject boundary gate, timetable, attendance, learning, exams/results, HR salary/payroll/leave, medical, storage adapter, provider buckets, notification delivery, AI/search projections and Flutter. The later admissions process may carry an applicant through configurable application, verification, test, interview, approval and fee-outcome stages without creating a student prematurely. Its approved enrollment handoff supplies a distinct student/admission identifier allocation and class placement command; no direct dependency on a Finance primary key is imposed. Timetable and attendance consume offerings and assignments later; they do not own them.

## Non-negotiable domain invariants

1. A student and an employee are separate profiles of one existing Person where applicable. Neither creates another human/authentication identity. A teacher who is a parent can have an individual staff principal and a distinct shared-family principal; grants, audit actors and data contexts never merge between them. Family actions are attributed to the shared principal, not a guessed adult.
2. Stable internal UUID, school Student ID, admission number, admission serial/sequence and enrollment roll number have different purposes and uniqueness scopes. Age and current enrollment summaries are derived. Final allocation ownership and sequence scopes are D1B blockers.
3. Student lifecycle and enrollment lifecycle are distinct. A status transition retains effective date, reason, verified actor and recorded time. Enrollment placement in year/campus/class/section/roll remains reconstructable after promotion, repeat, class/section change, transfer, withdrawal, re-enrollment or graduation. New placement never overwrites the old authoritative placement.
4. No historical enrollment/status/assignment is hard-deleted through normal operations. Corrections preserve old fact, new fact, reason, actor, approval when required and audit. Academic-year/default-year changes and archive never hide authorized history or reuse an archived offering as current.
5. **CONFIRMED:** class capacity is enforced for a class offered in a campus and academic year. A full class normally blocks enrollment; an authorized override is possible only if school policy allows it, with reason and audit. A section may have an additional independent limit, never a replacement for class capacity. D1B must settle count semantics, exact physical location, any additional section limit, and whether override also requires approval. Concurrent claims must serialize or enforce an equivalent invariant; no read-then-write race or MAX-plus-one numbering.
6. **CONFIRMED:** a section supports one designated class-teacher responsibility at a given effective time and distinct section-specific subject-teacher assignments across its subjects. Different sections may have different teachers for the same subject; one teacher may serve multiple sections, classes, subjects and campuses through separate effective assignments. Historical replacements remain. Assignment is contextual evidence, not a grant by itself. `role + action + complete grant + matching scope + assignment + target state + workflow` must all pass at operation time. Visibility does not imply attendance, marks, export or correction authority. Campus, year, section and subject ancestry are independently checked; an unknown resolver fails closed, never widens to ALL.
7. Private domain files reference Foundation `file_objects` through typed, owner-checked relationships. Server entitlement, enabled module, deployment-owned purpose, validated bytes and current domain authorization are separate gates. Existing private files remain after entitlement downgrade; neither object key nor signed URL is authority. Exact purpose activation remains TBD.
8. Foundation approval orchestrates only registered typed domain operations. Student/enrollment/assignment domains validate intent and apply results; no arbitrary JSON patch or direct authenticated DML. Sensitive profile corrections, status transitions, transfers, capacity overrides and assignment corrections require reason/history/audit; approval policy details are gated. Audit and outbox are recorded with successful domain effect and command receipt where applicable.
9. Offline reads are encrypted and scoped; queued writes carry stable intent/idempotency and expected versions, then recheck current authority and current capacity/state on replay. Promotion, transfer, roll allocation, status, relationship/access and teacher assignment cannot use last-write-wins. Server remains authoritative. No full offline protocol is selected here.

## Student and enrollment operation semantics

| Operation | Student lifecycle | Enrollment/history effect |
|---|---|---|
| Initial enrollment | Requires a student created by an authorized admission or direct-school intake path; applicant alone is insufficient. | Create first placement with year, campus, offering/section and roll allocated by protected command. |
| Promotion | Student may remain Active subject to policy and finalized-result input from later Results. | Close/complete old placement; create a new-year placement linked to its source. A failed/repeat/manual outcome is an explicit decision, not a blind grade increment. |
| Repeat | Does not create a second student. | New placement in the appropriate year, potentially the same class definition, with its own roll and reason. |
| Class or section change | Usually leaves student lifecycle unchanged. | End old effective placement and start a new one, retaining the prior class/section. Validate both scopes and capacity. |
| Campus transfer | Transfer of placement is distinct from a terminal student “Transferred” state; policy must specify internal versus external transfer. | End source-campus placement, create authorized destination placement for an internal move; external exit may end placement without destination. |
| Withdrawal | Student status may become Withdrawn under approved transition policy. | End active placement, retain its year/campus/section/roll history. |
| Re-enrollment | Requires approved transition from a non-active state where allowed. | Create a new placement; do not reopen or overwrite a historic one. |
| Graduation/expulsion/deceased | Status transition is separately evidenced. | No contradictory active placement may remain after effective terminal transition, subject to D1-08 transition decision. |

Student status answers school relationship; enrollment status answers a particular placement's participation. Reports must count effective placements for rosters and effective student statuses for lifecycle statistics, not substitute one for the other. D1-07/D1-08 gate precise overlap and invalid-combination rules.

Normal protected commands may update non-sensitive configuration/profile details with version checks. Exceptional capacity placement needs elevated permission and audit; whether it also needs approval is D1-06. Sensitive profile correction, status transition, transfer, enrollment correction, family-access change and retroactive teaching assignment correction need a reason, retained history and audit, with the configured approval requirement decided before activation. Domain validators, not Foundation's generic request payload, decide whether each proposed change is legal.

## Reporting, search and AI contract

Current roster and status summaries derive from effective history rather than replace it. Authorized historical queries specify year and campus context; broad search, exports, notifications and AI use the same row and field restrictions as direct reads. Identity documents, disability/orphan indicators, guardian contact details, employee private information and future medical/payroll facts need separate field-level disclosure review. A visible student row is not a license to disclose all profile fields. No read model or index is approved by D1A.

## Later package handoffs

Admissions hands off approved applicant intent to an idempotent student/enrollment command. Curriculum must bind its own subject/curriculum identity to academic offerings after the boundary decision; student optional selections and assessments remain with their later owner. Timetable consumes current teaching/room assignment and checks conflicts itself. Attendance, learning and results query historical enrollment/assignment context, without being allowed to rewrite it. HR/payroll consume employee identity and own compensation. Each handler must register typed permission, target, approval, audit and file-purpose contracts before activation.
