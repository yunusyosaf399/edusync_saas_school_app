## 2. Critical source-derived lock inversion — revise DB-04 strategy before any SQL

**Observed [SOURCE] D1 Enrollment END (direct and approved apply):**
1. End command obtains Foundation receipt/principal and current policy context. In the inspected final END public RPC it does **not** acquire an explicit School `FOR UPDATE` before `d1_enrollment_end_lock_context`.
2. End lock context locks `app_private.students FOR UPDATE`, then calls `d1_enrollment_place_lock_destination` (Academic context), then locks the `enrollments` source and one `roll_allocations` row.
3. `d1_enrollment_end_effect` updates the retained `roll_allocations.effective_until` followed by `enrollments.effective_until`.
4. Existing base `ab_d1_integrity BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON app_private.roll_allocations` calls `d1_guard_effective_history`; the *later* `effect30_33` body, for `roll_allocations`, requests **`school_profiles FOR UPDATE`** and then policy/allocator while the transaction already holds Student and academic/source history locks.

**Two-transaction counterexample [DERIVED, NOT EXECUTED]:**

| Phase | D1 END session E | Old proposed DB-04 selection session C |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `FOR UPDATE Student S` succeeds | — |
| 2 | — | `FOR UPDATE School H` succeeds |
| 3 | — | waits on `Student S` |
| 4 | UPDATE roll allocation invokes history trigger, waits on `School H` | still waits on `Student S` |
| 5 | **Wait cycle E→H→C→S→E**; PostgreSQL deadlock detection could abort one transaction | same cycle |

This is **a concrete source-supported potential inversion**, not proof that a runtime deadlock occurred. The earlier DB-04 design's blanket `Foundation→School→Student→D1 Academic→Curriculum` mutation order **MUST NOT** be implemented unchanged. The D1 END command/trigger may also be relevant to an *existing* D1 school-first Student writer; that is **D1-owned review**, not DB-04 repair authority.

### Candidate safe split of Curriculum lock plans [PROPOSED]

- **Curriculum revision publication/configuration and Section-subject offering changes (no Student mutation):** Foundation auth/command/reviewer prefix → **School `FOR UPDATE`** → sorted D1 Academic Class→Campus→Academic Year→Class Offering→Section Offering→Subject required ancestors → DB-04 definition→revision→applicability→group→rule→section-offering locks. **Never lock a Student or D1 Enrollment in this transaction**, including through accidental `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` across a JOIN or hidden FK/trigger. Read affected choice counts under serialized Curriculum applicability and reject incompatible cutover; use a separate authorized Student-scoped transition protocol later if CUR-06 approves one.
- **Student subject initial/change/withdraw/correct (staff-applied):** Foundation auth/command/reviewer prefix → **Student `FOR UPDATE` (no `school_profiles FOR UPDATE`)** → exact accepted D1 Enrollment `FOR UPDATE` or required key-share lock (fixed strategy TBD) → D1 Academic Class→Campus→Academic Year→Class Offering→Section Offering→Subject in sorted rank → DB-04 definition→revision→applicability **shared contention anchor**→group→rule→section-offering→selection lineage. Source and destination must be pre-discovered and sorted; recheck after every wait. Use no roll-policy/roll-allocator write or School row `FOR UPDATE`. Check school identity via normal read (not lock) and frozen ancestry.
- **Student/FAMILY proposals if authorized later:** same Student-first path; any affected Family rows are locked after Student following D1 Family ordering. Do not synthesize a Student/FAMILY authority from relationship/assignment.
- **Cross-domain stabilization:** publication and selection *both* lock the exact existing affected **Curriculum applicability** row(s) in compatible order, so publication sees selections committed before its fence or selects a newer incompatibility; Student commands that follow a cutover see stale revision and deny. If creating a brand-new applicability with no predecessor row, serialize revision coverage using a pre-existing definition/revision parent + unique overlap backstop. Do not rely on an unlocked count/query or synthetic “global resolver purpose”.
- **Avoid reverse FK edges:** DB-04 Student history references School only *transitively* through Curriculum/D1 IDs; no direct selection→School, selection→roll allocator or late FKs to a School row. No INSERT/UPDATE trigger from DB-04 choice history may silently acquire School `FOR UPDATE` after Student. The physical DDL/FK graph must be independently examined for that property.

**Limit of candidate split:** This **avoids adding the documented School↔Student inversion** if implemented as written. It is not a proof of safety in the presence of D1/DB-04 workflow steps, implicit FK `KEY SHARE`, deferred triggers, other D1 operations or unknown approved transitions. On discovering a required School lock from a Student-scoped path, **stop; coordinate D1 and Curriculum lock redesign** rather than swallowing PostgreSQL deadlock exceptions.

Ordinary FK enforcement does **not** prove same-school/class/year/campus ancestry when UUID FKs connect independently, nor enforce interval overlap. Never claim a `CHECK` validates a join.

Use existing PK plus protected campus validation; no new D1 key addition in DB-04. Foundation `campuses(id,school_id)` is likewise **not** a reviewed candidate key; do not add a fabricated composite FK. D1 immutable enrollment has **no row_version**; expected Student version + complete source Enrollment snapshot is the fence.

**Candidate protection for exact lineage:**
1. `student_subject_selections` can use FK `(enrollment_id,student_id) → enrollments(id,student_id)` (physically reviewed in the base SQL), plus separately Student PK if needed; compare source `section_offering_id` to the selected DB-04 offering's D1 Section via protected locked lookup.
2. Published child tables may be immutable after first publication, but **closing** published applicability/section-subject availability must use a one-way end marker + `closed_at/receipt` so business-time and knowledge-time reconstruction survive.
3. `UNIQUE(revision_id,subject_id)`, `UNIQUE(curriculum_id,revision_no)` and definition school+code are straightforward candidate B-trees. `UNIQUE(section_offering_id,subject_id)` cannot be enforced unless `subject_id` is deliberately copied as a **server-verified immutable consistency key** onto `curriculum_section_subject_offerings`; otherwise use locked join-scoped overlap validation and reconsider a redundant immutable subject key at SQL freeze.
4. Group occupancy cannot be protected by per-row uniqueness alone. Serialize **Student + applicability/group anchor**, scan current/future segment endpoints, enforce nonoverflow and finalized minimum even with multiple commands/same date. Include overlapping future elections and an empty group's min.
5. New-use archive denial must be at the **apply** transaction, not just draft validation: Subject can archive after validation. Retain already-published revisions/old Exam snapshots; whether active grandfathered choices survive Subject archive remains CUR-06.
6. Do not let a generic FK lookup under `FOR UPDATE` with multiple JOINs lock unintended later-ranked School/Student targets. Use explicit `FOR UPDATE OF alias` or individual purpose-built queries with confirmed relationship and row-lock levels. The final SQL reviewer must inspect DEFERRABLE constraint-trigger execution at COMMIT and FK cascading/RESTRICT timing.
7. Ordinary write permission for `authenticated`/`anon` remains none for all seven `app_private` relations; grant fixed vetted private commands/checked reads through approved deployment-owned permissions only.

## 4. Effective-time fencing and publication vs Student choice

**[PROPOSED] Publication** acquires School and Academic/Subject parents, then immutable DB-04 revision/applicability anchor. Before finalizing, it queries impacted accepted selection intervals **without locking Students**, evaluates whether replacement would orphan a choice at any relevant Student/Enrollment segment, and either rejects or relies on a **separate approved** transition operation. No optimistic “notify students then fix later”; historical Exams keep previously observed snapshots. It stamps immutable content digest + recorded publication time and dated applicability; no past row rewrite. If multiple sections affected, acquire their Class/Section/Subject identifiers in defined type/UUID order.

**[PROPOSED] Selection** locks Student + precise D1 Enrollment snapshot, Academic parents and the existing revision/applicability fence, then validates complete current permission + Subject/section state, eligibility and group cardinality **after waiting**. Its canonical intent binds current revision digest/expected selection head, no arbitrary client “purpose”. For accepted COMPULSORY, derive eligibility from rule+Enrollment; no fake Student selection row. For OPTIONAL/ELECTIVE, append accepted choice with retained supersession lineage. At source Enrollment END or MOVE, effective eligibility always intersects the *true D1* `[effective_from,effective_until)`, never follows a prior-year Student-choice row. If current D1 Enrollment ends before an accepted future choice, the effective read clamps to Enrollment and new changes deny; an explicit Student transition plan may later reconcile that pending choice, but must not rewrite it silently.

**[DERIVED] Publication–selection linearization:** because neither publication nor selection writes D1 canonical Enrollment, and publication never locks Student, both may converge on a **stable DB-04 applicability row**. Publish-first → selection wakes and rejects stale revision/context. Select-first → publish wakes and detects newly accepted potentially orphaned choice; reject unless reviewed transition covers it. In the zero-predecessor first-publication race, no selection can have previously valid applicability; exact definition/revision uniqueness and newly published coverage must be enforced at INSERT. This requires a real concurrency test and a proven commit-boundary digest check.
